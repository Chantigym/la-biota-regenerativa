Discover the project

Community

La Biota is an organic vegetable project run by women at El Mantillo, a rural plot in the heart of Traslasierra, Córdoba.

The project rests on two pillars: conserving the that still remains on the plot and producing food while respecting local biodiversity. Its premise is that soil health shapes the health of plants, animals and people.

Principles

Collective organisation
Production and financial decisions are made jointly, and the work is shared among members.
Gender perspective
We promote women's participation in farming, a field where they have historically been under-represented.
Transparency
Management methods, progress and the project's limitations are recorded and shared publicly.
Local consumption
We see buying local as a political stance: it shortens the distance between growers and eaters and strengthens the regional economy.

The project

The farm is organised into five interdependent sub-projects: the residues and surpluses of each one become inputs for the others.

  • Vegetable gardens

    Seasonal organic vegetables grown without synthetic agrochemicals, with crop rotation, permanent soil cover and on-farm compost.

    See details of Vegetable gardens

  • Eggs

    Pasture-raised laying hens whose diet is supplemented with grass, invertebrates and vegetable scraps.

    See details of Eggs

  • Chickens

    Broilers in rotational grazing with mobile coops, spreading organic fertility across the beds.

    See details of Chickens

  • Preserves

    Surplus vegetables processed into preserves, jams and pickles, reducing harvest losses and adding value.

    See details of Preserves

  • Organic pantry

    A shop bringing together our own produce and that of local growers with compatible practices.

    See details of Organic pantry

Soil biota

The project is named after the community of organisms living underground. Scroll down through the layers of a living soil.

Layer 1 of 5

Cover

Plant cover and mulch protect the soil from erosion, regulate its temperature and keep it moist.

  1. Layer 1

    Cover

    Plant cover and mulch protect the soil from erosion, regulate its temperature and keep it moist.

  2. Layer 2

    Roots

    Roots build soil structure and release exudates that feed the microbial community of the .

  3. Layer 3

    Soil fauna

    Earthworms, beetles and other invertebrates break down organic matter and dig channels that improve infiltration and aeration.

  4. Layer 4

    Fungi

    Mycelium networks, including , extend the reach of roots and help them take up water and nutrients.

  5. Layer 5

    Microorganisms

    Bacteria and other microorganisms decompose organic matter and make nutrients available. They are the the project is named after.
